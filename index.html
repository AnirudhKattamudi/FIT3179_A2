Chapter 1: Australia Through the Camera

Photographic observations provide a window into how Australians document the natural environment. But has photographic recording activity increased?

Key finding: Summed photographic observation counts increased by approximately 51.3% between 2023 and 2025. This reflects an increase in recorded activity, not necessarily an increase in biodiversity.

Source: Atlas of Living Australia (ALA) , photographic observation records, 2023–2025. Counts are summed across selected taxonomic groups and may not represent unique observations.

Chapter 2: Nature Through the Seasons

Australia's natural environment changes throughout the year. Does photographic recording activity follow a seasonal pattern? Explore monthly records and switch between years.

Key finding (2025): Nature photography records peaked in October at approximately 343,000 observations, compared with around 146,000 in June. Recording activity increased sharply during September and October, suggesting that spring is a particularly active period for documenting Australian nature. However, this reflects photographic recording activity rather than changes in actual biodiversity abundance.

Source: Atlas of Living Australia (ALA) , monthly photographic observation records, 2023–2025. Counts are summed across selected taxonomic groups and may not represent unique observations.

Different nature groups may have different seasonal recording patterns. The heatmap below compares each group's monthly activity with its own busiest month. Select a year to explore how these patterns change.

Key finding (2025): Most nature groups experienced their highest photographic recording activity during spring, particularly October. However, fungi followed a different seasonal pattern, with recording activity peaking around May. This contrast highlights how photographic observation patterns vary between nature groups and seasons, rather than directly measuring changes in biodiversity.

Source: Atlas of Living Australia (ALA) , monthly photographic observation records, 2023–2025. Counts are summed across selected taxonomic groups and may not represent unique observations.
