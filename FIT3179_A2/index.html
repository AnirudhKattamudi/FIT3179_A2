Chapter 2: Nature Through the Seasons

Australia's natural environment changes throughout the year. Does photographic recording activity follow a seasonal pattern? Explore monthly records and switch between years.

Key finding (2025): Nature photography records peaked in October at approximately 343,000 observations, compared with around 146,000 in June. Recording activity increased sharply during September and October, suggesting that spring is a particularly active period for documenting Australian nature. However, this reflects photographic recording activity rather than changes in actual biodiversity abundance.

Source: Atlas of Living Australia (ALA) , monthly photographic observation records, 2023–2025. Counts are summed across selected taxonomic groups and may not represent unique observations.

Chapter 4: Photography and Protected Landscapes

Australia contains thousands of protected natural areas, but does having more protected areas correspond with greater photographic recording activity? This scatter plot compares the number of protected-area features recorded in CAPAD 2024 with photographic observation density across Australian states and territories. Select a photography year to explore the relationship.

Key finding (2025): The number of protected-area features does not show a consistent relationship with nature photography density. Victoria has the highest protected-area feature count and relatively high photographic activity, while the ACT records by far the highest observation density despite having relatively few protected areas. This suggests that photographic recording activity may be influenced by factors beyond the number of protected areas, including accessibility, population concentration and observer participation.

Sources: Atlas of Living Australia (ALA) , photographic observation records, 2023–2025; CAPAD 2024 , terrestrial protected-area feature counts; and Geoscience Australia , state and territory land areas. Observation density is measured per 1,000 km². The vertical axis uses a symmetric logarithmic scale. Protected-area feature counts are not equivalent to protected-land coverage. This comparison is exploratory and does not establish causation.
